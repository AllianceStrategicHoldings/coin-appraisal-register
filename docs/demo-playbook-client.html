Demo Playbook — Coin Appraisal Register

Written 2026-07-26 against the live production app (https://coin-appraisal-register.vercel.app) running on your own Supabase, Cloudflare R2, Make.com and GoHighLevel accounts. Updated 2026-09-01 for the restructured intake, the relocated under-18 stop, required customer details with payment detail, grading on every coin type, the locked Customer View, and the live GHL fan-out.

Purpose: a guided tour of the app in five scenarios. The "Why it works this way" sections explain the design decisions behind each behaviour.

PART 0 — Before the demo

0.1 Installing the app on an iPad (from scratch)

  1. Open Safari (it must be Safari — other browsers can't install web apps on iPadOS) and go to https://coin-appraisal-register.vercel.app.
  2. Tap the Share icon (the square with the up arrow) → Add to Home Screen → Add.
  3. Close Safari and launch the app from its home-screen icon. It runs full-screen with no browser bars — this is the mode reps should always use.
  4. The first time you tap Take photo, iPadOS asks for camera permission — tap Allow.
  5. Updates ship automatically: fully closing and reopening the app picks up the latest version. No App Store, no profiles, nothing to manage.
  6. After the first load the app works with no signal — completed deals queue on the device and send themselves when connection returns.
  7. Recommended for customer handoffs: enable Guided Access (Settings → Accessibility → Guided Access). Triple-click the side button before handing the iPad over and the device is pinned to the app; the in-app Customer View lock hides your numbers, Guided Access additionally stops the customer leaving the app.

0.2 Two things worth knowing up front

① Demoing the returning-customer lookup needs a customer with history. There is none in the database — it's clean. So during the demo: run Scenario 1 first, then start a new intake and type the same phone number (the lookup matches on phone alone). The banner appears with the deal that was just done. That's a stronger demo than seeded data, because the history is created live, not staged.

② The bullion premiums are placeholders, and they make the app offer ABOVE melt. Example: 1 American Gold Eagle = 1.0 ozt × $4,051 spot = $4,051 melt, plus the placeholder $75 premium = $4,126 offered. Spec 2.2 defines bullion as "unit × spot + premium", so the maths is right — but $75 is an invented number, and a real buy-side premium is usually much smaller or negative. Realistic buy premiums can be set in config_coin_types at any time — these are placeholders until your real numbers go in.

0.4 Resetting between scenarios

The cleanest reset is finishing a deal and pressing New Customer — that clears the intake, the bag and the deal state.

If you need to reset mid-deal, in Safari: Settings → clear website data for the site, or use a Private tab. What's stored:

  • car.intake.v1 — the intake draft (session)
  • car.cart.v1 — the bag
  • car.deployment.locationId.v1 / .eventId.v1 — location + event mode
  • car.dealQueue.v1 / car.approvalQueue.v1 — offline queues

Event mode is sticky — it stays on until you turn it off in the Deployment panel. Check the header chip before each scenario: grey = store mode, indigo = event mode.

SCENARIO 1 — The everyday deal

~8 minutes. The core loop — the spine of the demo.

Goal

Show a complete, ordinary transaction end to end, and land the two ideas that matter most: the customer walks in fast but a deal can't close without the full audit trail, and the customer never sees your margin.

Steps

  1. Open the app. It lands on Customer Intake — a light gate now: name, phone (optional but powers the returning-customer lookup), email (optional, flows to your CRM), Reason for coming in = Estate, How did they find us = Google, and the marketing consent checkbox.
  2. Watch the "Missing:" line — only name, reason and consent gate the calculator. When it empties, Open Calculator turns green.
  3. Choose Test Rep in the Rep dropdown.
  4. Add Coin → Morgan Silver Dollar → quantity 20 → Add to Bag.
  5. Stop and read the four tiles: Total Value, Max Payout, Actual Offer, Delta.
  6. On the line, tap the Offer $ field and negotiate down (e.g. from $268 to $240). Watch Delta and the margin % move.
  7. Review Deal → the rep summary. In the Deal notes (optional) section, fill in the two rep-entered analytics fields: "When did the customer acquire the items?" and "Competitor offers received?" with the amount if known.
  8. Tap Customer View → and hand the iPad over. Let it sit for a beat — then try to swipe back. Nothing moves: the view is locked. Tap Employee View → enter a wrong code (rejected) → 1234 → you're back in your numbers.
  9. Accept → the customer details step: DOB (the calendar opens and stays open while you pick — an adult date), zip 74104, driver's licence number, DL photo.
  10. Payment method: Check → a Check number field appears — enter one (e.g. 1047). Wire and Other ask for their own detail; Cash does not.
  11. Take the final lot photo, then have "the customer" sign.
  12. Complete Deal → Deal Complete. Within seconds the deal is also in your GHL trigger history — accepted deals hit the accepted hook.
  13. New Customer, then type the same phone number. The blue "Returning customer" banner appears with the deal just completed, and name / zip / licence pre-fill.

Key points

"Intake takes ten seconds — a name, a reason, a consent box — so the line keeps moving. But nothing skips the audit trail: the deal physically cannot complete until ID is photographed, the licence number is typed, the payment is documented and the customer has signed."

At step 8:

"This is the same screen. One tap. Your margin, the max payout, the negotiation delta — all gone. And it's locked: the customer can hold the iPad and there is no swipe, no gesture, no path back to our numbers without the employee passcode."

At step 13:

"Same customer walks back in next month. The rep types the phone number and immediately sees what we paid last time — before quoting anything."

Why it works this way

  • The audit gate moved to the close (operator decision 2026-08-23): the calculator opens on name + reason + consent, but completing a deal requires DOB, zip, licence number and photo, payment method with its detail, the lot photo and the signature. Speed at the door, rigour at the close.
  • Consent lives at intake so declined customers are contactable too — the walk-aways are exactly who your follow-up campaigns need.
  • Max Payout vs Actual Offer is the dual pricing engine (spec 2.4). Max Payout is the calculated ceiling — melt × your margin. Actual Offer is what the rep actually offers. The gap is the Delta: negotiation room the rep captured, and the number you'll use to measure them later.
  • The customer view hides margin by rendering a different component, not by hiding elements with CSS. The wholesale price, comps, Max Payout and delta are never sent to that screen — there is nothing on it to reveal, and the passcode lock (2.5) means there's no way off it either.
  • The offer letter PDF is generated on the iPad, with the signature embedded above the signature line, then uploaded to your storage. It works with no signal.

Watch out for

  • The photo upload takes a second or two; wait for "Uploaded ✓" before moving on, or it looks like it hung.

SCENARIO 2 — The guardrails

~10 minutes. The four expensive mistakes the app refuses to allow.

Goal

Show that the app stops a rep from making the four expensive mistakes, and that a manager — not the rep — decides when to proceed.

Steps

A. Under-18 hard stop (fires at the close)

  1. Run a quick deal to the Accept step, and in Date of birth pick a post-2008 date, then close the picker.
  2. The screen goes full red: "Cannot Proceed."
  3. Show the two exits: Entered the date wrong — re-enter (a typo escape that keeps the whole deal intact) and End Deal — Start New Customer. There is no third option and no override — an under-18 date can never complete a deal.

B. Sterling hallmark check 4. New customer, reach the calculator. 5. Add Coin → Sterling Silver. 6. The hallmark popup fires the moment you select it, before any weight can be entered. Note the STERLING / 925 guidance and the EPNS warning. 7. Tap Hallmark confirmed — continue, enter a weight, add it.

C. Pre-1933 US Gold 8. Add Coin → $20 Double Eagle (Pre-1933) — note the red MANAGER tag next to it in the list. 9. Full-screen red stop. 10. Manager Override → enter PIN 1234 → the item unlocks, and the screen shows "Manager approved Pre-1933 US Gold ✓".

D. Offer above Max Payout 11. On any line, type an Offer higher than its Max Payout. 12. It's refused with "Above Max Payout — requires a manager override code."

E. $9,500 cash stop 13. Add 500 g of 14K Gold (~$11.4k) → Review Deal → Accept. 14. Choose Cash → full-screen red $9,500 Cash Stop. 15. Enter a wrong PIN first (9999) — it's rejected. 16. Enter 1234 → cleared, and "Manager approved cash over $9,500" is recorded on the deal.

Key points

"Four different ways a rep can cost you money, and the app stops all four. Notice the manager PIN isn't checked on the iPad — it goes to your database, matched against a hash. The PIN is never stored in plain text and never travels in plain text."

On D:

"The rep physically cannot offer more than the ceiling. Today that's a hard block. Once the override-code mechanism is in, a manager will be able to issue a one-time code for a specific deal — that's the next piece of work."

Why it works this way

  • The under-18 stop rides on the DOB, which is collected at the close — so that's where it fires. DOB is a required completion field precisely so the stop can't be skipped by leaving the date blank. The stop waits for a committed date, so the calendar picker can't trip it mid-selection, and the typo escape means a fat-fingered year doesn't cost the rep the deal.
  • The gates fire on selection, not on save. If the hallmark popup appeared after weighing, the rep would already have anchored on a number.
  • PIN checking is server-side. The app sends the PIN to a Make scenario which SHA-256s it and matches reps_master.pin_hash. If the check can't be reached, it fails closed — no PIN passes. A stolen iPad can't approve anything offline.
  • Every acknowledgment is written to the deal record with a timestamp — pre1933_gold_ack, cash_over_9500_ack. Manager approval is auditable after the fact, not just a click that vanishes.

SCENARIO 3 — Expert pricing

~8 minutes. Shows the app knows more than a calculator.

Goal

Show the three outside price sources feeding one decision, and what happens when they come back empty.

Steps

A. Cert lookup that works

  1. Add Coin → Morgan Silver Dollar (Graded).
  2. In Certification number, type 30000001 → Look up.
  3. Read the result: PCGS confirms 1923 $1 · MS64; CDN wholesale $125.00 appears beside Our offer $37.50 at 30% margin; eBay sold comps show low/median/high with the number of sales.
  4. Add to bag. Note the line is priced from the lookup, not from a grade multiplier, and its quantity is fixed at 1 — one cert, one coin.

B. Any coin can be slabbed 5. Add Coin → Mercury Dime — the Grade row now appears on every coin type (optional here, required on the numismatic items). 6. Tap Slabbed → the same cert-number entry and manual wholesale override slide in. Point out this works on every coin in the list.

C. The fallback chain 7. In that panel, enter cert 99999999 → Look up. 8. "PCGS has no record of this certification number." 9. A Manual price override field appears. Type 200 → it shows the offer at your margin ($60.00). 10. Clear the field → the Ask Manager escalation appears instead.

D. Other item types (quick tour) 11. American Gold Eagle 1 oz — the picker shows +$75.00 premium; bullion is priced spot + premium, not spot × margin. 12. Other Foreign Silver — purity is prefilled at 0.800 and is editable; change it to 0.900 and watch the price move. 13. Paper Money — denomination / year / condition, and the rep types the offer directly. The manager-review note is always on screen.

Key points

"Three independent sources: PCGS authenticates the slab, Greysheet gives the dealer wholesale, and eBay tells us what these actually sold for in the last 90 days. The rep sees all three next to the offer."

On C:

"This is the part that matters operationally. When the price sources come back empty, the app doesn't guess and it doesn't stall — it steps down: Greysheet, then a manual price, then a manager. Every deal has a next step."

Why it works this way

  • Grading is open to every coin type (spec: "extended to all graded categories") — any coin can be slabbed, so Slabbed opens the cert flow everywhere. Scrap by weight and paper money are the only exceptions.
  • Cert entry is manual by design. Spec 2.3 explicitly excludes a barcode scanner SDK, so the rep types the number off the slab.
  • CDN price and your offer sit side by side so the rep can see both the market and the position at a glance — that's the spec wording.
  • Empty comps are a valid answer, not an error. A scarce coin with no recent sales says "No recent sales found", because showing an error there would train reps to ignore errors.
  • A real wholesale price beats a grade multiplier. The multiplier is a generalisation; a Greysheet price is for that coin at that grade — so when a lookup resolves, it prices the line.

Watch out for

  • The lookup takes ~8–10 seconds because it's three APIs in sequence.
  • PCGS rate-limits rapid lookups, so it's done once per run-through.

SCENARIO 4 — Roadshow, managers, and no signal

~8 minutes. The operations story.

Goal

Show the app handles a travelling business and a bad venue connection.

Steps

A. Location & event mode

  1. Tap the location chip in the header (top, next to Refresh Config).
  2. Choose Dallas — Main Store. Note every deal on this iPad now carries that location.
  3. Under Roadshow event mode, choose Tulsa Coin Show → manager PIN 1234 → the chip turns indigo and reads Event: Tulsa Coin Show.

B. Ask Manager 4. Back on the calculator, tap Ask Manager (it's on every deal screen). 5. Type a note — "customer claims this is a mint error". 6. Send to manager → the whole deal freezes. The calculator is genuinely unreachable, with a live timer running. 7. Manager — Resume Deal → PIN 1234 → back to where you were.

C. Not Sure What This Is 8. Tap Not Sure What This Is → photograph the item → type "eagle" in the reference search and show the matches from the coin list.

D. No signal 9. Put the iPad in airplane mode. 10. Complete a deal all the way to Complete Deal. 11. It succeeds and says the record is queued — "will send automatically when connection returns." 12. Turn signal back on, then reload the app. The queue flushes — and the deal reaches both your database and your GHL hook.

Key points

"At a roadshow the venue Wi-Fi is always terrible. The rep never sees a failure — the deal completes on the iPad, the offer letter is generated on the iPad, and the record sends itself when signal comes back. Nothing is lost and nothing is blocked."

On A:

"In event mode we also measure how far the customer travelled from the venue, not from your Dallas store — because at a roadshow, distance to Dallas tells you nothing."

Why it works this way

  • Location is a per-device setting. Each iPad is deployed somewhere, so it's stored on the device rather than chosen per deal — one less thing for the rep to get wrong.
  • Entering event mode needs a manager PIN; leaving it doesn't. Turning tagging on changes how deals are attributed; turning it off is harmless.
  • Ask Manager truly freezes the deal. It's a full-screen state, not a banner — the rep can't keep negotiating while waiting.
  • The offline queue is the same mechanism for deals and manager requests. Both retry when the app next starts.
  • Reload is required to flush the queue (it runs on app start).

Watch out for

  • Turn event mode off afterwards, or later demos will still be tagged.
  • Step 12: the flush happens on app start — reload, don't just wait.

SCENARIO 5 — Your accounts, your data

~5 minutes. Short, and it closes the ownership argument.

Goal

Prove the six ownership conditions you set are actually true.

Steps

  1. Supabase → Table Editor → deal_log. Show the deal just created: totals, spot prices captured at that moment, payment method and payment detail (the check number), the manager acknowledgments, and all five analytics fields — selling_reason, referral_source, estimated_collection_age, competitor_offers_received (with competitor_offer_amount), and customer_zip_radius_miles.
  2. Open deal_line_items — every item on that deal, with grade and cert number where captured. customer_master holds the profile, including the new email field.
  3. webhook_deliveries — the fan-out audit: for that deal, one row per destination (primary_db and ghl), each marked delivered with a timestamp. Failed sends are retried automatically every 15 minutes.
  4. GoHighLevel → your webhook trigger history — the same deal, seconds after completion, with customer name, phone, email, totals and the analytics fields ready for your follow-up campaigns.
  5. config_coin_types. This is the "edit without a developer" moment: change Silver margin in config_margins from 0.30 to 0.35, then in the app tap Refresh Config and re-add a coin. The offer changes. Change it back afterwards.
  6. Cloudflare → R2 → coin-appraisal-register-production. Show the lot photo, licence photo, signature PNG and the offer-letter PDF from the deal — open the PDF.
  7. Make → the scenarios, and the run history showing the deal.

Key points

"Everything you're looking at is in your accounts. Your Supabase, your Cloudflare, your Make, your GHL. I'm a collaborator and you can remove me at any time. Nothing about this build is hostage to me."

On step 5:

"Margins, coin types, premiums, rep records — all editable here by you, in a spreadsheet view, no developer involved. That's what the Loom walkthroughs will cover in detail."

Why it works this way

  • Nothing is in a black box. The database holds data and integrity rules only; all the logic runs in Make where you can read it step by step.
  • Full export any time — CSV per table, or a full SQL dump. The data is never trapped.
  • Rep PINs are stored as hashes, so even with full database access nobody can read a PIN.
